[Bearing capacity](../reference/glossary.md#bearing-resistance) is the pressure under a footing at which the ground fails in shear and the footing punches in. It grows fast with the soil's [friction angle](../reference/glossary.md#friction-angle), and more slowly with the footing's width and depth.

**You'll use:** [effective stress](../reference/glossary.md#effective-stress) and [friction angle](../reference/glossary.md#friction-angle).

## The idea

When a footing is pushed down, a wedge of soil under it moves down with it and shoves the soil beside it sideways and up. The ground resists in three ways: cohesion along the slip surface, the weight of the soil beside the footing that has to be lifted, and the friction from the weight of the soil inside the moving wedge. Each gets a bearing capacity factor, and the three add up:

$$
q_{ult} = c'\,N_c + q'\,N_q + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\gamma'\,B\,N_\gamma
$$

Here q′ is the effective overburden pressure at the footing's base, γ′ the effective unit weight of the soil and B the footing's width. The factors N depend only on the friction angle.

> [!note]- Learn more: where N_q comes from
> Prandtl worked out the slip surface for a weightless soil around 1920: a straight wedge under the footing, a logarithmic spiral beside it, and a straight wedge at the surface. Reissner added the overburden in 1924. The result is
>
> $$N_q = e^{\pi \tan\varphi'}\,\tan^2\!\left(45^\circ + \varphi'/2\right)$$
>
> The exponential is why bearing capacity is so sensitive to the friction angle: N_q grows by a factor of 1.7 to 1.9 for every 5° between 25° and 40°. Eurocode 7 Annex D uses this N_q and N_γ = 2 (N_q − 1) tan φ′ for a rough base.

## Explore it

The cells below are a [marimo](../tools/marimo/index.md) notebook running in your browser. A strip footing on dry sand with a unit weight of 18 kN/m³ and no cohesion. Move the friction angle from 30° to 35° and watch the result roughly double.

```python marimo
import marimo as mo
import numpy as np
```

```python marimo
phi = mo.ui.slider(20, 40, step=1, value=30, label="Friction angle φ′ (°)", show_value=True)
width = mo.ui.slider(0.5, 4.0, step=0.25, value=1.5, label="Footing width B (m)", show_value=True)
depth = mo.ui.slider(0.0, 2.0, step=0.25, value=0.75, label="Depth of the base D (m)", show_value=True)
mo.vstack([phi, width, depth])
```

```python marimo
gamma = 18.0
r = np.radians(phi.value)
n_q = np.exp(np.pi * np.tan(r)) * np.tan(np.pi / 4 + r / 2) ** 2
n_gamma = 2 * (n_q - 1) * np.tan(r)
overburden = gamma * depth.value
q_ult = overburden * n_q + 0.5 * gamma * width.value * n_gamma
mo.md(
    f"N_q = {n_q:.1f} and N_γ = {n_gamma:.1f}, so the bearing capacity is "
    f"**{q_ult:,.0f} kPa**: {overburden * n_q:,.0f} kPa from the overburden "
    f"and {0.5 * gamma * width.value * n_gamma:,.0f} kPa from the soil's weight in the wedge."
)
```

## In design

Eurocode 7 doesn't use q_ult directly. It divides the soil's strength or the resistance by partial factors, and adds factors for the footing's shape, the load's inclination and the base's depth. The [spread foundations](../geotechnical-structures/spread-foundations.md) page lists the other checks a footing needs. To write these steps up in a [calculation report](../reference/glossary.md#calculation-report), [SymEval](../tools/symeval/index.md) prints each formula with the values and units that went in.
